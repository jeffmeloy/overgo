outcome: the block's forward graph and the backward graph derived from it run on the device as they
are, and train there as on the host: over ten updates of an adapter on a small block with grouped
attention every loss is the same to a part in a hundred thousand, and the loss falls. Beside the
trainers the program would replace the measurement is mixed, and where the comparison is like for
like it is against the program.

Against the LoRA trainer, which is the only adapter trainer and runs on the host: a step of the
program on the device takes 10.3 ms where the LoRA trainer's share for a block is 163 ms, a sixteenth.
That sets a device against a host. On the host itself, through the reference executor, the program's
step takes 343 ms, about twice the LoRA trainer's.

Against the resident trainer, which trains every weight on the device with a forward and backward
written by hand: the program's forward and backward take 4.6 ms and the resident trainer's share for
a block 2.2 ms, so the derived passes take about twice as long, though they differentiate by less (the
factors, not the weights). The rest of the program's step, 5.7 ms, is nearly all Muon's update of the
fourteen factors on the host, the update the LoRA trainer takes; the whole step is about as long as
the resident trainer's share for a block, 10.7 ms, most of which is its update of every weight on the
device. The program peaks at 118 MB of device memory (101 MB in another run: the graph's order is not
fixed), of which 60 MB are the block's weights and the rest a step's activations and gradients, among
them a transposed copy of each weight; the resident trainer peaks at 305 MB on a model of one block
and adds 192 MB a block. It holds every weight's gradient and Muon's state on the device, which an
adapter's training does not, so the two memories are not one comparison.

Where the time goes. A step's graph is 666 operations, of which 517 are launched (the rest are inputs
and reshapes); the forward alone launches 46. The attention's backward, composed a query head at a
time from two-dimensional products, is 26 launched operations a head, 364 of the 517 at fourteen
heads. The passes are bound by that number, about nine microseconds an operation: replaying the
launches as one captured graph (the executor's retained execution) was tried and left the passes
between 4.2 and 4.8 ms, where launching them one by one took 4.4 to 4.9. What would bring the derived
passes to the written ones is an attention backward over all heads at once, which the operations
composed here do not give.

How each figure was taken. Times are the mean of 64 steps on the device and 3 on the host, after one
step that is not timed. The LoRA trainer and the resident trainer train whole models, so each was run
on models of one and of five such blocks, and a block's share is a quarter of the difference; the
resident trainer was run once to load its kernels before the run measured, and its forward and
backward are its own synchronized measurement. Memory is the device's peak for the process's one
worker. Times are in microseconds and memory in bytes.
